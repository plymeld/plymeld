# Test models

The models and reference files the tests read. Everything here may be redistributed: the 3DBenchy is CC0, the rest is
generated by or for plymeld. Reference values (`*.reference.json`) were computed once, independently of plymeld, and
are committed; the tests compare plymeld against them.

Most tests build their models in code (boxes, ramps, arches) and need nothing here. `tools/regress.sh` slices the
models below with the sample configuration in `test-models/config/` and the plan files in `tools/regress/`.

## Two-color 3DBenchy: the default multi-color model

| file | source | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `3DBenchy-dual.3mf` | CreativeTools/3DBenchy's two halves, downloaded 2026-09-28, as one project, a part per filament: A, `Multi-part/#3DBenchy - Dualprint - Hull, Box, Bridge walls, Rod-holder, Chimney - (3DBenchy.com).stl` (139,542 triangles, closed, 3 shells, 12,938.0 mm³); B, `Multi-part/#3DBenchy - Dualprint - Gunwale, Deck, Plate, Wheel, Frames, Roof, Chimney top - (3DBenchy.com).stl` (122,978 triangles, closed, 21 shells, 2,619.7 mm³, z 5.15..48.00) | CC0 1.0. The two-color tests: `MultiPartTest`, `ThreeMfReaderTest`, `ToolAssignmentTest`, and those reading its parts (`benchyDualParts()` in plymeld-check's tests) |

Measured independently of plymeld (2026-09-28):
- A and B share one frame: x −32.2..27.8, y −30.5..0.5, z 0..48, not the single-part file's placement. The project's
  parts are the two halves' meshes unchanged (every vertex within 0.000001 mm, read with lib3mf, 2026-11-02).
- A + B = 15,557.7 mm³ against 15,550.5 mm³ for the single part.
- **The overlap is about 6.1 mm³** (sections at 0.2 mm layer mid-heights), but 4.93 mm³ of that comes from two layers,
  z 8.9 and 18.7, where flat faces of both halves lie within about 0.01 mm of the plane and the reference misreads the
  section. On the other layers the overlap is 1.17 mm³ of slivers where the halves meet; plymeld agrees (1.13 mm³,
  `FilamentAreasTest`). plymeld has one tie layer of its own: z 8.5, where B's deck top (491 mm², facing up) lies
  exactly on the plane while A starts at 8.498, so a 0.002 mm sliver reads as a whole layer (37.8 mm²).
